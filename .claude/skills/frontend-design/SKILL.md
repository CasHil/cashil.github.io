# Frontend Design

Approach this as the design lead at a design studio known for giving every client a distinct visual identity that is not mistaken for anyone else's. This client has already rejected proposals that felt cliché or templated, and is paying for a distinctive point of view: make deliberate, opinionated choices about palette, typography, and layout that are specific to this brief, and take aesthetic risk if justified.

## Ground your designs in the subject matter
The subject's industry, subject matter, materials, and vernacular are where distinctive visual choices come from. Build with the brief's real content and subject matter throughout.

## Design principles
- **Hero**: Open with the most characteristic thing in the subject's world. Avoid default tropes like big generic number with small label and gradient accent unless justified.
- **Typography**: Typography carries the personality of the page. Use intentional weights, widths, and spacing. The type treatment itself is an active part of the design.
- **Line Length**: Default to line lengths of less than 80 characters.
- **Avoid default typographic tells**:
  - Accenting a single word or phrase with gradient or fake bolding.
  - Adding unnecessary decorative labels or pill badges above content.
  - Using all-caps pill buttons for basic actions.
- **Visual structure is information**: Structural devices like outlines, borders, numbering, and dividers encode useful information about the content rather than decorate it.
- **Real Sequence**: Use numbered or connected markers only if the content really is a sequence — like a chronological career timeline.
- **Restraint in Motion**: Avoid scattered fade-and-slide-up entrances and hover transitions on every card. Motion should answer user actions.
